Research Interests

I am an ethnographer of work and dwelling. My research asks how the people who sustain urban economies, among them street vendors, kitchen workers and brewers, navigate the conditions under which they labour and live, and how those two things fold into one another.

My master's fieldwork took place inside a natural brewery in Yunnan, China, where I developed competitive collaboration as an analytic framework for working relationships sustained not through consensus but through continuous, friction-filled adjustment between actors of unequal power sharing a space under uncertainty. Before that, I spent three months alongside internal labour migrants selling street food in Suzhou, tracing how livelihood and spatial precarity intersect under informal urban governance.

I am currently developing a project on the nexus between labour and housing in Amsterdam's food service industry, asking how the everyday routes and rhythms of migrant workers reveal the ways employment and housing precarity intersect.

Research and Outputs

2024 to 2025 · Yunnan, China · Solo researcher

“Let the Wild In”: Fermentation, Cleanliness, and Competitive Collaboration in a Multispecies Brewery

Universiteit van Amsterdam · Supervised by Dr Rebeca Ibáñez Martín

For three months I worked as an assistant brewer in a small natural brewery in Yunnan, studying how brewers and microorganisms negotiate a shared space. The expertise I wanted to document lived in smell, touch and timing rather than in anything my participants could state directly, so I joined the work rather than observing it from outside, building field access across linguistic and ethnic boundaries through sustained embedding and reflexive positionality work.

I traced how cleaning practices, temperature decisions and moments of deliberate non-intervention constituted a relationship neither party fully controlled. From this I developed competitive collaboration: an analytic framework for working relationships sustained not through consensus but through continuous, friction-filled adjustment between actors of unequal power sharing a space under conditions of uncertainty.

2022 · Suzhou, China · Research Fellow

From the Street to the Night Market: Informal Economy and the Regulation of Street Vending

Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University · Supervised by Dr Lefeng Lin · Fully funded by the University Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship

Three months of qualitative fieldwork with internal labour migrants working as street food vendors during the COVID-19 lockdown, tracing how livelihood and spatial precarity intersect under informal urban governance. Participants were men and women across age groups who had migrated from less-developed regions to sustain themselves in the urban informal economy.

I built access by becoming a regular customer and gradually entering vendors’ everyday networks. Early on a community gatekeeper feared the research would trigger governmental repression of their trade; I worked through this by being transparent about the study’s policy orientation. Mapping where vendors lived revealed that they clustered in the same area, an early version of the participatory mapping I now propose to use in Amsterdam.
